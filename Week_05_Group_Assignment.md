## Build a 3D game

Your group may use AILab or another accessible coding/agent harness. Research and choose a model and harness that fit your objective, then state your reason briefly in the report. Skills, plug-ins and MCP connections are optional; use them only when they help your game. No special skill bundle, paid tool or new account is required.

1. **AI ALLOWED — define the game.** What does your group want to build? Agree on its setting, player goal, controls and win/lose or scoring outcome. Keep the scope your group can finish by the deadline. You can brainstorm with AI to get some ideas.
2. **AI REQUIRED — build with a harness.** Use **HTML, CSS, JavaScript and Three.js** to make a browser-based 3D game in `w05_3d_game/`. At minimum, the game must have a real 3D scene, an appropriate game camera, player control or meaningful interaction, a clear objective, visible progress or outcome feedback, a start/restart path, and a short complete play loop that can be demonstrated within five minutes. **One complete level is sufficient. The entire game may take five minutes or less to complete; it does not need to last longer than five minutes. Multiple levels are optional.** A still scene or fly-through is not enough. Name the harness and model in the report, explain briefly why they fit the job, disclose material AI help, and preserve one representative prompt plus a short AI response or resulting change. State what your group checked before keeping that result. You may work with AI to choose optional skills or plug-ins that help your objective. Once the minimum game, sound and demo mode work reliably, and your group has completed the external playtest, two improvement cycles and required video, extra levels, visual polish and additional features are optional.
3. **AI ALLOWED — add sound.** The game must include audible feedback that supports play, such as movement, collection, collision, success, failure or background music. Include an easy volume or mute control. Sound may begin after the player clicks or starts the game when the browser blocks autoplay. Use only audio your group created or has permission to use, keep required audio files inside `w05_3d_game/`, and credit outside or AI-generated audio in the report.
4. **AI ALLOWED — collaborate.** Work inclusively as one team. The game receives a group result, but every member must have a named contribution and personally run at least one game check. If you use AILab, use AILab's group push/pull controls. You may use GitHub.com, but a public repository is not required. If you use GitHub, put the repository URL in `W05_GroupID.md`; submit the actual game files even if the repository is private.
5. **AI ALLOWED — test, improve and package.** Each member should run the game. Check the controls, objective, feedback, sound, short play loop and restart. AI may suggest tests, diagnose problems or help implement changes, but your group must decide what to keep and must run the game again after each change. Package the files needed for the documented local run; gameplay must not depend on a live AI service, account, remote backend or other external service. Put the controls, exact run/setup steps, tested browser, sound control and known limits in `README.md`. Use only assets you have permission to use; credit outside assets and disclose material AI help.
6. **AI REQUIRED — implement demo mode.** Add a clearly labeled **Demo** or **Watch Demo** button on the game's start or menu screen. After one click, the submitted game must automatically run a short, representative gameplay sequence without further player input. The demo must use the actual game—not a prerecorded video—and show the objective, meaningful interaction, visible feedback and audible game sound. A scripted path or simple bot is acceptable; machine learning is not required. The demo must stop or return to the start/menu screen, and the user must be able to exit or restart it and then begin normal human play. Test this behavior from the submitted files and explain how to use it in `README.md`.
7. **NO AI — run an external playtest and capture authentic feedback.** Ask at least one person who is **not a member of your registered project group** to play the game. Give the tester only the normal starting information and controls, then let the person try the game without coaching; help only if the game cannot continue. Afterward, ask what was clear, what was confusing or difficult, and what one improvement they suggest. In `W05_GroupID.md`, state your intended player/customer segment and record an anonymous tester label plus a short, non-identifying profile relevant to that segment: broad age range, general role or context, gaming frequency or experience, and familiarity with similar games. State whether and why the tester fits your intended segment. Also record the date, what the tester tried, what your group directly observed, and the tester's exact words or your faithful notes. Do not include an exact age, school, employer, class/section, name, student ID, contact details or unrelated sensitive information. Obtain permission to include the anonymous profile and notes. If the tester permits, your group may record the playtest as a video for your own study. Recording is optional, the playtest video is **not required for submission**, and it must not be included in the submitted homework package. Do not record video, audio or photos without the tester's permission. Do not use AI to generate, translate, rewrite or polish the tester's profile, feedback or your observations.
8. **NO AI — record authentic iteration evidence.** In `W05_GroupID.md`, record at least **two** short improvement cycles. At least one cycle must begin with the external playtest from Task 7 and show what your group changed in response and what happened when you retested. For each cycle, state what your group personally observed, what you changed, how AI helped if applicable, who worked on or checked it, and what happened when you retested. Do not use AI to generate, translate, rewrite or polish these observations. Keep an honest failed or partly successful attempt if it helped the next decision.
9. **AI REQUIRED — OPTIONAL AI agent playtest.** After normal human play and the required in-game demo mode are working, you may let an **external AI agent play the game**. This is different from Task 6: do not press the game's Demo button and call that AI play. The AI agent must observe the rendered game, decide what action to take next and operate the game controls itself. At minimum, this normally requires browser/computer-control tools that can see the game screen and send keyboard or mouse input. Your group may give the agent the objective and start the run, but do not manually steer during the AI-play segment. If you show this experiment in the required video, label it **AI PLAY** and keep it separate from the required human-play segment. Name the AI tool under material AI help. This task is optional and ungraded; skip it if no accessible tool supports browser/computer control. It requires no paid tool or new account and cannot replace the required in-game demo mode or human-controlled gameplay.
10. **AI ALLOWED — make an audience-centered promotion video.** For this presentation scenario, assume a promotion committee from a large gaming platform will review **only your video** to decide whether your team and game may enter its promotion program. The platform has tens of millions of players aged 15–25, and the committee will spend no more than five minutes on your video. Your actual course submission still includes the report and runnable source, but this committee sees neither. It judges both **team quality** and **game quality**.

    First decide who matters. The committee is the decision audience, but “players aged 15–25” is too broad to be a useful player audience. Identify the specific segment within that range most likely to care about your game—consider motivations, genre preferences, experience level and play situation—and make the video meaningful to that segment. Show the player outcome or experience you promise and why your team can deliver it. If the video is mainly a list of features, you are probably going in the wrong direction. Features matter only when the video connects them to a player benefit and demonstrates that benefit through the real game.

    You may use AI to analyze the committee and player audiences, challenge assumptions, compare possible narratives, draft or revise the presentation, plan the edit, and critique a rough cut. Ask AI to identify what evidence would make skeptical judges trust the team and want the game on their platform, then make your own decisions and disclose material AI help. AI must not fabricate gameplay, tester feedback, team contributions or results.

    Use the five minutes to make a focused case, not to cover everything. Introduce the team and game; establish a strong player-facing hook; demonstrate a short complete play loop, responsive control, audible sound and the game’s quality; briefly show the in-game Demo button and automated demo mode; and show how authentic external feedback and iteration made the experience better. Give the committee credible evidence of team ownership, judgment and ability to improve—not merely a list of member names or tools. The video may combine clear screen capture with over-the-shoulder footage, but it must include at least one genuine human-play segment showing a real group member operating the controls while the game responds. A face does not need to appear; hands using a keyboard, mouse or controller are sufficient. Demo mode, screen capture and an optional clearly labeled AI-play segment from Task 9 cannot replace human play.

    Submit one MP4 named `W05_GroupID_Game_Demo.mp4`, **5:00 or shorter**. One member submits the single group copy. Record only consenting people, and do not show passwords, private messages, unrelated personal information or another person's screen.
